# Phase 1 results: DSP signals vs benchmarks (walk-forward, net of costs)

> Hypothetical, simulated research on historical data. **Not investment advice.** No orders were placed.
> Generated by `scripts/phase1_research.py`; rerun it to reproduce. Config hash `f3a197826d91fca1`.

Setup: daily total-return closes (`adj_close`), rolling walk-forward with 756 training bars and 126 test bars. In each fold the configuration with the best net in-sample Sharpe is held out of sample. Costs are 4 bps per side plus 50 bps/yr borrow on shorts. Trades are filled one bar after the signal. Benchmarks use fixed textbook parameters (no selection) and are measured over the same out-of-sample window. Buy-and-hold is always long-only.

DSR = deflated Sharpe ratio (probability that the true Sharpe is > 0 after accounting for 190 trials and the spread of trial Sharpes). The usual bar is DSR > 0.95.

## Summary

* Walk-forward DSP results: 40 (4 families x 2 modes x 5 symbols).
* Beat buy-and-hold on Sharpe: **4 / 40**. Beat the best benchmark on Sharpe: **2 / 40**.
* Pass the deflated-Sharpe bar (DSR > 0.95): **1 / 40**.
* Median out-of-sample Sharpe: WF-DSP 0.14; buy-and-hold 0.57; SMA 50/200 0.34; TSMOM 0.35.

A DSR above 0.95 says a Sharpe ratio is unlikely to be pure luck given the number of trials. It does **not** say the strategy beats buy-and-hold: a long-flat trend filter on an index that rose over the period mostly earns the same market exposure, with less of it.

### Caveats

* Five liquid ETFs, one data vendor (Yahoo), daily bars, about 13 years out of sample: a small sample. Results are not evidence of future performance.
* Costs are a flat bps model. There is no market impact model, and ETF dividends are taken from the adjusted close without withholding tax.
* The grids are small and were fixed before this run. No grid was tuned after looking at these results.
